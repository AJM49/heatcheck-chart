WHAT IS THE HEAT INDEX?

Billboard ranks who’s biggest. The Heat Index (0–100) ranks who’s moving fastest. It has three parts:

  • Chart position — up to 40 points. The higher you rank, the more points. #1 earns 40; #25 earns about 2.
  • Movement — up to 40 points. Every spot you climb earns 10 points. Climb 4 or more spots and you max out at 40. Falling earns no momentum points.
  • Instagram follower growth — up to 20 points. Grow your Instagram followers 5% or more between editions and you max out at 20. This is the only audience number in the score.
Formula: Heat Index = Chart position score (26 − Rank) ÷ 25 × 40  +  Momentum score (spots gained × 10, capped at 40)  +  Instagram follower growth score (edition-over-edition Instagram follower growth % × 4, capped at 20). Total is capped at 100 and floored at 0.

The Heat Index is live. Week 2 scores are real — ILLIT’s 88.0 is the highest on either chart. One quirk of this edition: follower growth is measured against the Week 1 snapshot (Oct 14, 2026), a 30-day span, because this chart’s second edition took longer to land than planned. New entries earn position and growth points but no momentum points — they have not climbed yet. The Biggest Mover crown goes to the highest Heat Index on each chart.

HOW THIS CHART IS BUILT

Week 2. Charts fetched Nov 13, 2026 and compared against the Week 1 snapshot (Oct 14, 2026). Movement shows spots gained or lost vs. Week 1; Peaks carry across editions; Weeks counts editions charted. Artists who fell out of the Top 25 are out of this edition.

Hip-Hop source. The official Billboard Hot 100 playlist on Spotify (fetched Nov 13, 2026), filtered to hip-hop, R&B & Afrobeats acts — lead and featured artists both count, one entry per artist at their highest-charting song. Note the Halloween effect: Michael Jackson’s “Thriller” (playlist #20) and Rockwell’s “Somebody’s Watching Me” (playlist #39) re-chart on seasonal streams and are counted as R&B catalog entries.

K-Pop source. Spotify Top 50 — South Korea (fetched Nov 13, 2026), Korean acts in chart order, one entry per artist at their highest-charting song. The one non-Korean act in the Top 50, Post Malone & Swae Lee’s “Sunflower” (#49), was skipped. RESCENE holds four songs in the Top 50; their entry rides their highest, “With” at #2.

Audience numbers — what counts, and what doesn’t. Two audience figures appear in the tables, and only one of them touches a score. Instagram followers (from Instagram’s own system, Nov 13, 2026) feed the Heat Index: the growth component compares each artist’s count with the previous edition’s snapshot — this edition, Oct 14 → Nov 13. Counts were accepted only where the profile was verified and clearly matched the artist. Spotify monthly listeners (from Spotify, pulled Nov 13, 2026; the same audience figure Spotify shows on every artist page) are printed for context only — monthly listeners are not an input to the Heat Index or to the forecast model, and no score would change if the column were removed. Spotify does not publish artist follower counts through its data tools, which is why this chart shows monthly listeners rather than a followers figure it cannot verify. Where a number could not be verified it is shown as “unknown” and counts as 0 — never estimated. Two notes this edition: Jimin’s Instagram count is published for the first time (his verified account is active; with no Week 1 baseline, his growth score is 0 this edition), and CORTIS’s Instagram count shows unknown because it could not be re-verified this edition — a stale number was not carried over.

Forecast. The Next Week Forecast comes from a logistic-regression model trained only on this chart’s own history — its two inputs are an artist’s chart rank and the size of their Instagram audience (log scale). No outside data is fed to it, and Spotify monthly listeners play no part in it. Its limits and track record are printed in the forecast section, where they can be checked.
