ABOUT HEAT CHECK

Billboard ranks who's biggest. Heat Check ranks who's moving fastest.

What this is

Heat Check is a weekly momentum chart for mainstream hip-hop and K-pop. Instead of ranking artists only by how big they already are, it tracks who is climbing — new entries, fast risers, and the artists gaining followers the fastest — and rolls that movement into a single score.

Heat Check is published by @almogul, the music-discovery project behind the Fresh Voltage playlist. It is not affiliated with Billboard or Spotify.

The Heat Index (0–100)

Every artist gets a Heat Index score built from three parts:

  • Chart position — up to 40 points. The higher the rank, the more points. #1 earns 40; #25 earns about 2.
  • Movement — up to 40 points. Every spot climbed earns 10 points. Climb 4 or more spots and you max out at 40. Falling earns no momentum points.
  • Follower growth — up to 20 points. Grow your followers 5% or more in a week and you max out at 20.
Formula: Heat Index = Chart position score (26 − Rank) ÷ 25 × 40 + Momentum score (spots gained × 10, capped at 40) + Follower growth score (week-over-week follower growth % × 4, capped at 20). Total is capped at 100 and floored at 0.

Week 1 shows "—" for Heat Index because there is no prior week to compare against. The score activates in week 2, and the Biggest Mover crown goes to the artist with the highest Heat Index each week, starting week 2.

How the charts are built

Hip-Hop Top 25. Built from the official Billboard Hot 100 playlist on Spotify, filtered to hip-hop, R&B, and Afrobeats acts — lead and featured artists both count, one entry per artist at their highest-charting song. Billboard's genre-chart pages could not be read directly for this build, so ranks follow playlist order except where noted, and the page says so.

K-Pop Top 25. Built from Spotify Top 50 — South Korea, Korean acts in chart order, one entry per artist at their highest-charting song, cross-checked against Circle Chart, Instiz, and Melon.

What is not guessed. Spotify follower counts are listed as "unknown" for all 50 artists because Spotify's tools expose monthly listeners, not follower counts — nothing is invented to fill the gap. Instagram counts are published only where the profile was verified and clearly matched the artist. Where the two sources disagree, the page says so.
